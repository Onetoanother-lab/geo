# Cinematic QA — Phase 2

This is an editorial and implementation audit of the existing twelve chapters.
The fourteen-part emotional arc is documented in [CINEMATIC_SCORE.md](CINEMATIC_SCORE.md)
and implemented in `src/content/cinematic.ts`. Memorability below is a design
judgment, not a claim from an audience study.

## Scene review

Each chapter was reviewed against the ten questions in the Phase 2 brief:
focal point; motivation for motion; meaning; distinction; transition; emotional
purpose; stillness; contrast; reduced motion; and the image worth remembering.

### I — Opening / the fall

1. **Focus:** the isolated tree, then the space it occupied.
2–3. **Motion:** a slow approach reveals depth; birds leave; the tree accelerates under gravity. The small impact response locates the event without dominating it.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** the richest composition becomes quieter; exposed roots carry the second statement into the ecosystem cross-section.
6. **Arc:** wonder → curiosity → anticipation → disruption.
7. **Stillness:** two separate creaks, a pause before movement, and a timed 1.8-second post-impact hold before the first statement.
8. **Contrast:** pale narrative text over layered dark trunks; optional projector shadow lift.
9. **Reduced motion:** the standing tree changes to a stump through opacity; no rotation, approach or impact shake. Both statements and their timing remain.
10. **Memory:** the missing tree and the delayed recognition that it was connected to others.

### II — Living system

1. **Focus:** the relationship selected by the audience.
2–3. **Motion:** the soil section is uncovered; selected flows explain ecological connections. Repeating hotspot rings were removed.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** a cross-section replaces the forest vista; the same branching language continues into the clearing and its water corridor.
6. **Arc:** understanding.
7. **Stillness:** no automatic hotspot selection or competing camera movement during exploration.
8. **Contrast:** labelled controls and foreground text; the portrait caption is positioned directly after the diorama and remains scrollable when expanded.
9. **Reduced motion:** opacity replaces the soil reveal; connections and native buttons remain usable.
10. **Memory:** one tree sharing a system with water, soil, wildlife and air.

### III — Degradation

1. **Focus:** the clearing frontier.
2–3. **Motion:** tree cover, wildlife, insect detail, water shimmer, particles and shade withdraw at different thresholds. The sound layers follow the same normalized condition.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** an increasingly exposed landscape replaces the dense system; stump rings become the radial cause composition.
6. **Arc:** concern.
7. **Stillness:** stopping the slider stops the ecological transformation; the chosen sentence completes its timed entrance.
8. **Contrast:** narration and qualitative indicator labels remain separated from the pale exposed ground by text shadows and restrained scrims.
9. **Reduced motion:** tree crowns disappear without rotating; a keyboard range provides the same progression.
10. **Memory:** loss communicated by missing life, not a red tint.

### IV — Causes

1. **Focus:** the circular forest patch under one selected pressure.
2–3. **Motion:** canopy changes show what a selected driver does. Unselected drivers remain still.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** a compact radial composition interrupts the broad vista; radial paths become fractures.
6. **Arc:** concern becomes a question about mechanisms.
7. **Stillness:** this chapter waits for a choice.
8. **Contrast:** warm labels on dark fields; long phone labels wrap within the pressure ring instead of clipping.
9. **Reduced motion:** state changes and descriptions remain available through the original controls.
10. **Memory:** several different pressures acting on the same finite patch.

### V — Consequences

1. **Focus:** one causal chain at a time.
2–3. **Motion:** the connecting line and successive nodes express sequence; tabs choose a different mechanism.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** the graphic is deliberately sparse after the radial patch; its branching lines lead toward the river/leaf scale sequence.
6. **Arc:** concern gains an understandable structure.
7. **Stillness:** completed chains remain available to read.
8. **Contrast:** simple pale labels and bounded icons; data references retain the existing facts/source links.
9. **Reduced motion:** no node scale pop; the same chain and text remain.
10. **Memory:** an ecological effect continuing beyond the cleared boundary.

### VI — Scale / world

1. **Focus:** a leaf vein becomes a river-like branch, then geographic lines and a world silhouette.
2–3. **Motion:** shared SVG topology changes scale and meaning; geographic illumination appears only after the shape resolves. Forest regions then light one at a time (Amazon, Congo, Southeast Asia, boreal); the case places join quietly with the controls. A selected region dims the others.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** the nearly dark field opens into global geography; country cases lead toward the Central Asia and Uzbekistan approach.
6. **Arc:** scale.
7. **Stillness:** no orbiting globe, animated pins or automatic region carousel.
8. **Contrast:** the title and source panel have separate safe areas; the overview carries a single sourced forest-share value.
9. **Reduced motion:** shape states and opacity convey the analogy without camera travel; all region buttons remain.
10. **Memory:** the resemblance between leaf veins, rivers and geographic connections.

### VII — Country cases

1. **Focus:** one country silhouette and its sourced land-cover comparison.
2–3. **Motion:** existing in-world fill and historical progression explain change, not decoration.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** individual places replace the global overview; the Uzbekistan case leads into the bundled Central Asian geography.
6. **Arc:** local relevance.
7. **Stillness:** explanatory text remains ordinary readable copy.
8. **Contrast:** existing country fills and light typography are retained; source references stay with each comparison.
9. **Reduced motion:** the existing accessible static states and controls remain.
10. **Memory:** similar forest percentages can conceal different ecological histories and conditions.

### VIII — Aral

1. **Focus:** the exposed horizon, followed by sparse saxaul patches.
2–3. **Motion:** bundled country boundaries establish location through one camera (world, Central Asia, Uzbekistan) that zooms evenly toward its target and never swings it off-centre; the explicitly schematic sea diagram gives way to dusty ground; vegetation interrupts the dust locally.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** horizontal, bleached and empty after dense forest chapters; restored roots lead into a seedling and the simulator.
6. **Arc:** emptiness → possibility.
7. **Stillness:** silence on arrival, long open intervals, sparse text and wind-dominant optional audio.
8. **Contrast:** dark text on pale land; source detail remains grounded in the existing facts.
9. **Reduced motion:** direct shoreline states, no descending camera, opacity for plant arrival.
10. **Memory:** a stranded vessel on the exposed seabed; planting is partial protection, not a returned sea.

### IX — Simulator

1. **Focus:** the selected patch and the consequences of its proposed use.
2–3. **Motion:** only changed tiles and indicator levels respond. Water color and canopy detail reflect condition.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** the audience now acts; a shared seedling/branch motif carries that result into recovery.
6. **Arc:** agency with tradeoffs.
7. **Stillness:** no countdown, score celebration or automatic decision-making.
8. **Contrast:** desktop time controls sit beneath the landscape; smaller projector layouts preserve the disclaimer and tradeoff explanation. Narrow layouts scroll naturally.
9. **Reduced motion:** the grid works without its entrance animation; radio tools and roving keyboard focus remain.
10. **Memory:** protection, food production, selective forestry and infrastructure have different benefits and ecological costs. The experience remembers the choice.

### X — Recovery

1. **Focus:** one tree growing through selectable time rings.
2–3. **Motion:** branches, canopy, soil cover, surrounding structure and wildlife develop at different rates.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** the busy decision surface becomes a patient single-tree composition; its branching trunk divides into two futures.
6. **Arc:** patience.
7. **Stillness:** each ring is a place to stop; text entrances finish in real time without requiring continued scroll.
8. **Contrast:** the trunk and source note were lifted; phone controls have quiet dark backing and a stable three-column arrangement.
9. **Reduced motion:** no tree/crown scale growth or flying bird; the ring buttons and range preserve access to every stage.
10. **Memory:** a canopy can form before mature ecological complexity. Regrowth and intact mature forest are not equated.

### XI — Two futures

1. **Focus:** the boundary between two trajectories of the same place.
2–3. **Motion:** scroll advances environmental change; the audience moves the comparison seam.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** the single trunk becomes a shared branching composition; the healthy-side geometry matches the opening and the finale.
6. **Arc:** tension.
7. **Stillness:** a stationary seam holds a comparison; no winner animation.
8. **Contrast:** side labels and the illustrative disclaimer remain separate from the central comparison.
9. **Reduced motion:** state changes replace camera and growth movement; the native range supports keyboard comparison.
10. **Memory:** two possible environments occupying the same spatial composition.

### XII — Return

1. **Focus:** the familiar opening tree and water, then the final two statements.
2–3. **Motion:** environmental health and saturation interpolate from the remembered result and comparison, leaning toward the opening light unless the audience leans clearly toward loss; mist slows as interaction settles.
4–5. **Distinction / transition:** the split becomes one recognizable forest. The environment remains at the end of the document.
6. **Arc:** reflection.
7. **Stillness:** the interface recedes, near-silence precedes the statement, and the second line arrives after a pause. Ambience returns afterward.
8. **Contrast:** large pale text, restrained shadow and the same layered composition as the opening.
9. **Reduced motion:** the same statements, timing and comparison remain; no autonomous pulse or camera movement.
10. **Memory:** “O‘rmon kelajagi hali yozib bo‘linmagan.” followed by “Uni biz yozamiz.”

## Verification and limits

Results on 2026-10-05, production build, Windows laptop, Chrome 154:

- `npm run check`: typecheck, lint, 55 unit tests and the build pass.
- `npm run test:e2e`: all 18 browser tests pass. They capture every chapter at
  1920×1080, 1366×768, 1280×720, 1536×864, 390×844 and 820×1180 with no page errors
  and no horizontal scroll. They also measure the real post-impact silence in full and
  reduced motion, and check cinema-mode focus, narration on reverse scroll, simulator
  memory across a reload, the finale composition and presenter isolation.
- Mid-act reload (Acts III, VIII, X): the act is current and its stage stays pinned.
- `scripts/cinematic-audit.mjs`: fullscreen, mute, the motion toggle, wheel and
  trackpad input pass; offline reload renders the map, Aral, recovery, finale and
  opening; no external requests; no page errors. The synthesized touch gesture did
  not scroll in headless Chrome; the pre-Phase 2 build behaves the same and nothing
  in the page blocks touch. Touch still needs a check on a real device.

### Fixed during this QA pass

- The opening scroll hint could fade back in over the tree fall. Time now reveals it
  and scroll hides it through separate elements.
- The finale looked grey at the default comparison. Its health and saturation now
  follow the comparison and lean toward the opening light.
- All world regions lit at once. Forest regions now arrive one at a time.
- The Aral approach zoom swung the target toward a corner mid-move. It now uses one
  camera that zooms evenly toward its target.
- Phase 2 recomputes the forest palette inside each scene, so `--life` finally
  recolours the trees (before, the palette was resolved once at the page root). That
  made every scrub frame repaint every tree. Scrubbed timelines now write `--life` in
  0.02 steps (`src/lib/animation/life.ts`), and the director writes its values only to
  the elements that read them. With GPU rasterization at normal CPU speed, every act
  now scrubs at roughly 45–60 frames per second (Act III lowest). Before the fix, the
  two-futures act managed about 20.

### Performance limit

At 4× CPU throttling, Acts III, X and XI still produce frames longer than 50 ms while
scrubbing quickly. The pre-Phase 2 build showed the same pattern (measured side by
side), so this is not new, but it remains. On a weak presentation laptop, plug it in,
enable hardware acceleration and use reduced motion if scrubbing stutters.

### Other limits

The Aral shoreline remains an explicitly labelled schematic. No verified historical
shoreline series was bundled, so no historical boundaries were invented. Geographic
country outlines use the existing bundled Natural Earth data. Simulator state,
ecological mixing and the future comparison are illustrative, not predictive.

Physical projector black levels, room acoustics and ten-minute audience recall require
an actual presentation rehearsal. The presenter-only calibration screen supplies
shadow steps, text tests, a 16:9 safe area, resolution/motion telemetry, shadow lift
and an optional sound check for that rehearsal.
